Keep the ontology ​

The ontology is the permanent overview of your project: where it comes from, where it stands, where it is going. Its atoms are the development axes; they come before the evolution register. Each accepted proposal raises the ontology version, and the history tells what entered the roadmap.

When the agent acts on the Map ​

  • At the start of a session, it reads effortless_ontology_check. Three alerts tell it to act: closed work not cited (the Map does not know it is done), roadmap to arbitrate (a lane has nothing “now” while work waits “next”), arbitration requested (you clicked “Ask for arbitration”).
  • After each closure of a story, an epic or an evolution, the response says whether an atom cites the closed work. If not, the agent reads effortless_ontology_coverage (which suggests candidate atoms) and proposes the attachment.
  • The roadmap: on these alerts, it arbitrates (effortless_ontology_roadmap_arbitrate) and proposes its moves. You see them in preview; you accept, remove a move (× in the detail) before accepting, or reject with a reason.
  • Reorganising meaning always goes through a proposal; whatever destroys waits for you, with its effects.
  • The /effortless-carte command (installed by the deployment in Claude Code) runs this cycle: read, cover what was done, arbitrate, propose.

What the agent does ​

  • It never changes the schema directly: it proposes. In semi-auto mode the proposal waits for your approval; in full mode it is accepted at once, and the version goes up with its trace.
  • Backfill: on a project that already exists, Effortless gathers what the project holds (epics, stories, tasks, decisions, evolutions, global documents) and what the schema already covers, in lots: an epic scope, a range of epics, or the registre lot for evolutions without an epic. From each lot the agent draws one proposal of layers, atoms and links. Effortless invents nothing: the agent interprets, you validate. Run again, the backfill only picks up what is not covered yet, and it names the remaining lots. Each lot also shows the code its epics touched (the folders of their commits): the agent draws the components from it and links them to the capabilities they realise. A change is cited by its dated form (26EVO112, 26FCT349); those no citation names unambiguously are listed apart.
  • An atom goes through three review states: To review (added, not framed yet), Framed (a pending proposal frames it), Validated (framing proposal accepted; an editing proposal does not validate). An added atom, by you or by a proposal, is born "to review" and stays so until it is framed. Only validated atoms enter the development plan.
  • An atom's progress is never typed in: it is computed from the epics, stories, tasks, evolutions and decisions of its pipe. Without a pipe, it follows the links: a goal follows the capabilities that serve it, a component those it realises, a module the components it contains.
  • When the project has an ontology, promoting an evolution that is not an anomaly creates an ontology proposal, no longer an epic, whether the request comes from you or from the team loop.
  • Reorganising meaning: grouping, changing layer or merging goes through a proposal. merge_objects merges atoms: links, citations and work move to the kept atom. The propose response carries its effects; a proposal that destroys something (merge, removal of an atom, a link or a layer, a card leaving one of your groups) is never accepted automatically, even in full mode: it waits for you, and accepting it needs acknowledge: true, once you have seen what disappears.
  • The map carries no meaning: moving, grouping, colouring or rerouting a link never changes the version.
  • The agent arranges in its own layout. When you ask it to arrange the map, it creates a named layout "Proposition de l'agent — <date>" (or completes its own): its groups, colours and groupings live there, marked "agent". Custom and your work never move. It takes after your habits, which the engine reads for it: your palette, the colour you set per type and per layer, what the cards of each of your groups share. A card it puts into one of your groups stays a suggestion (dashed outline) until you keep it. You look at the proposal, then Keep all, Keep one element from the inspector, or Delete the proposal; keeping is undone with ⌘Z. Its last arrangement can be undone too. It also writes the route of its proposal: the steps in the order the project is thought through, each with a title, a sentence, and the groups or cards it shows. The guided tour follows that route instead of the ① → ⑦ thread; a step showing something that does not exist is refused, and nothing is written.
  • Your work is protected. Renaming, recolouring, moving into another group or removing one of your groups, changing a colour you set, taking a card out of one of your groups: the agent cannot. The engine refuses and names each element; the agent proposes it to you instead.
  • Your work first. Your groups, their names, their colours and the cards you put in them are shared by every layout: Districts, Circles and Nesting arrange them as one block, without touching them. A layout owns positions only. Adopting a layout, by you or by the agent, creates a layout of your own (“Districts edited”) and never overwrites Custom, the project's layout.

What you get ​

The file .effortless/ontology.json (stable, readable in a diff), its history .effortless/ontology_history.json, the proposals .effortless/ontology_proposals.json, the map .effortless/ontology_view.json (your work and Custom), your named layouts .effortless/ontology_layouts.local.json (yours, never versioned), and a readable rendering blueprints/7-Ontologie.md regenerated on every write.

When a call is refused ​

The refusal names the cause and the act that lifts it: invalid or already taken id, unknown layer or type, link against the base rules (for instance sert goes from a capability to a goal), second link of the same type between the same two atoms, atom still linked (pass cascade: true), unreadable pipe reference, priority outside 1 to 3, unknown or already settled proposal, backfill already pending (settle the current proposal first), unreadable lot, invalid colour or anchor side on the map, agent gesture on your work (ontology:travailUtilisateur, each element named), layout that is not the agent's (ontology:dispositionPasAgent). A proposal that no longer applies to the current schema is refused without writing anything: reject it, then propose an up-to-date batch.
