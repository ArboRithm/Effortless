Map ​

This screen is the map of your project: its development axes, arranged in layers and linked to each other, with their progress computed from the register. You come here to see at a glance where the project comes from, where it stands and where it is going, to arrange the map your way, and to settle what the agent proposes. It opens from Map, in the « Steering » group of the sidebar.

What you see ​

The screen reads in two zones. At the top, steering: the current version; the N agent proposals pill when the agent awaits an answer; To handle (with the number of items) and History, which open their panel beside the map; Undo the agent's arrangement when the agent has just arranged the map; Export. Left of the map, the element tree: the whole structure of the displayed map, full height — the zones and sub-groups of a layout, or the layers and groups of Custom, each with its number of cards. On the map, the visual: at the top, centred, Search…, the reading (Past, Present, Target) and Filters; the dock at the bottom, centred — atom types as icons (when a panel is open and room runs short, they fold behind Add), then Select, Group (F), Grid, Tour and the layout picker; at the bottom right, the minimap and the zoom — −, the percentage (a click returns to 100 %), +, Fit all and Display options. Each right-hand panel closes with its cross.

On the map:

  • in Custom, each layer is a frame; a group is a free frame you draw yourself. In a computed layout, frames are rounded zones, one per step of the thread, and the layer is only a discreet sub-group;
  • each atom is a card: its header, marked with its type's icon, is coloured by that type (goal, actor, capability, component, module, interface, data entity, flow, constraint, decision, risk, milestone), with its priority; a component's technical nature (screen, service, API…) shows in a badge, with its icon, next to its type; at the bottom, its review (To review, Framed, Validated) and its progress, computed from the epics, stories, tasks and changes of its pipe. What is done is shown faded, what is in progress stays solid. A ▲ flags an item to handle: a click on it opens it;
  • each link is a curve from the source atom to the target atom, with its type and nature. Its ends spread along the edges when several links leave the same side.

Filters opens five lists — Layer, Horizon, Progress, Review, Epic — and dims what does not match, hiding nothing: the map keeps its shape. Each active filter becomes a pill next to the button; its cross removes it, and only it. Three readings each answer a question, written next to their buttons: Past — “What have we delivered?” (what is done stays in colour, with the version that delivered it, “delivered in v0.2.47”, or “done, not delivered”); Present — “What is moving forward?” (what is in progress and what carries an alert); Target — “Where are we going?” (one colour per horizon, and the gaps: aimed at Now but not started, or aimed at without any work). The rest fades to grey. The legend counts each entry; a click on an entry keeps only it, a second click removes it.

What you can do ​

Every gesture is saved at once. ⌘Z undoes it and ⇧⌘Z redoes it, for every gesture of the session.

  • Navigate: the wheel or a pinch zooms around the pointer; hold Space and drag to pan; ⌘0 or ⇧1 fits everything (Fit all), ⌘1 returns to 100 %; the zoom buttons do the same. Zoom and framing glide smoothly; any new gesture interrupts them. The minimap, above the zoom, recentres the view on a click; click and drag to move it live. Seen from afar, each zone shows its name large, at its top left.
  • Select: a click on an element opens its panel; ⇧-click adds to the selection; a rectangle drawn on the background selects what it covers; Esc deselects; Delete removes the selection, after a confirmation that names what goes (« Delete 2 atoms and 3 links? »), like every deletion.
  • Arrange: drag an atom or a frame (a frame carries its atoms); a group is grabbed by its header, its background or its border, a layout zone by its border. When selected, a frame shows its size handle in the bottom-right corner. The element stays where you drop it. The map snaps to the grid (Grid, ⌘') and to the edges and centres of other objects; magenta guides show it, and equal gaps between neighbours are measured. Hold ⌥ to suspend snapping. Dropping an atom into a group puts it there; into a layer, changes its layer.
  • Choose a layout: the layout button, at the end of the dock, offers Districts, Circles and Nesting, then Custom and your named layouts. The first three arrange the map along the design thread — ① Goal → ② Capabilities → ③ Targets → ④ Modules → ⑤ Components → ⑥ Decisions → ⑦ Delivered capabilities, with the Periphery (risks, constraints, resources) and Off the thread (what has no link) apart: Districts as successive districts, Circles as a spiral around the modules, Nesting as nested boxes (Intent, Solution, Decisions). A pale numbered thread links the steps. These layouts are computed on display and write nothing; your choice is kept in your profile and does not change the map for others.
  • Your work first: your groups, their names, their colours and the cards you put in them are shared by every layout. Districts, Circles and Nesting arrange each of your groups as one block, in the step that holds most of its cards; a group without cards goes to Your groups. A computed box you renamed or coloured keeps its name and colour from one layout to the next; when a layout has no matching box, a banner says so, and nothing is lost. Renaming or colouring a group, colouring a card, creating a group apply everywhere, whatever layout is shown.
  • Keep a layout: a layout is created, it never overwrites anything. In a computed layout, Keep this layout, or simply moving a card, creates a layout of your own, “Districts edited”; a banner says so, with Rename and Undo. Custom is the project's layout, shared with the team: it only changes through a gesture made in Custom. Your named layouts are yours alone (they never go into the repository); the selector renames, duplicates or deletes them, and a deletion can be undone from its banner. A card a layout does not place yet arrives next to its neighbours, marked to place, until you set it down.
  • The agent's proposal: when you ask the agent to arrange the map, it creates its own layout, "Proposition de l'agent — <date>", marked "Proposed by the agent" in the picker. It takes after your colours and groups but never touches your work or Custom. A banner says what it proposes (groups, colours, groupings); a card it puts into one of your groups is marked suggestion. Keep all moves the proposal into your work; Keep, in the inspector, keeps one element only; ⌘Z undoes. Delete the proposal removes it.
  • Describe a group: in a group's panel, Metadata lists what the group carries; the + button opens a table where each row picks a metadata Effortless understands, then its value among what the project holds: an epic, a story, a decision, an evolution, a stack, a document, a horizon or a layer. The card on the right says what each metadata is for, what it links to, and what Effortless and the agent do with it. Nothing is invented: an unknown key is refused; an old key that is not understood is kept and flagged. The group shows its metadata at the end of its header (the state of the cited epic, for example), and a card created in the group receives its layer, stack, horizon and the citation of the work in its pipe; the banner says so.
  • Tour: Tour walks the thread step by step (Previous, Next, Quit, or Esc). The step is framed and haloed, the rest fades; a cartouche says what the step brings to the reasoning and, for each card, what it links upstream (a capability, the goal it serves). At step ⑥, each attached decision is read live from the registry: the decision, its motive, the rejected alternatives (struck through, as ghosts) and the periphery forces that weighed in; what is not recorded says so (“no motive recorded”). The tour writes nothing. On an agent proposal whose route it has written, the tour follows that route: one step per moment of the reasoning, in its order, with its title and sentence; each step shows its groups (cards and subgroups included) and the cards it names. The pale thread on the canvas then goes through these steps, numbered like the tour's counter.
  • Add: drag an icon from the dock onto the map, or click it to place the atom at the centre of the view; it is born To review. The Group (F) tool draws a group: drag on the background to draw a rectangle; the cards it surrounds belong to it, and moving it carries them. Released over a group, the new group becomes its sub-group. While a group is selected, every element added from the dock goes into it, at a free place, and the group stays selected for the next one. A group only touches how the map is presented, never the schema or the versions.
  • Create by linking: pull a handle and release it in empty space; a small window offers the elements compatible with the one you start from (from a component: realises → capability, exposes → interface, persists → entity…). Choose, name it, Create: the element appears there, already linked. ⌘Z removes both.
  • Link: hover an atom, pull one of its four handles to another atom; during the gesture, the atoms that can receive the link light up, the others dim. The New link window offers only the types the rules allow for this pair, minus those already linking the two atoms; choose the nature, a description, then Create link; a click outside closes it.
  • Route a link: select it to see its waypoints; drag them, double-click a segment to add one, a point to remove it; its ends can be dragged to another side. Its label slides along the link, and a little to the side; Recentre the label, on right click, puts it back in its automatic place.
  • Browse the tree: the tree scrolls on its own; its chevrons expand a frame. Choosing a card selects it, centres it and moves a little closer; choosing a frame frames it. The card selected on the canvas is highlighted in the tree. After a click in the tree, with the keyboard, ↑ and ↓ move from one row to the next and show the element reached on the canvas (a group reached unfolds); → expands a frame or enters it, ← folds it or goes up to the parent frame; Home and End go to the first and last row. Drag a card or a group from the tree onto a group to attach it there: it takes a free place, and the group grows if needed. The button in its header folds it into a thin strip, and its right edge drags to widen it (200 to 480 px); width and folding come back when you reopen.
  • Search: ⌘F puts the cursor in Search…; what you type also filters the tree, which keeps only the cards found and their frames, expanded; type a name (accents don't matter), an identifier or a registry reference. Choosing a result selects the atom, centres it and zooms in if it was too small to read.
  • Sort: Filters keeps one layer, horizon, progress, review or epic; Alerts › With an alert keeps only the atoms with something to handle. Each frame shows its number of alerts (▲).
  • Focus, collapse: right-click a frame, Focus on this frame dims the rest (the Focus chip or Esc leave it); the chevron at the start of the title, or Collapse, shrinks the frame to a bar (“4 atoms collapsed”) that its links reach; Expand opens it again.
  • Validate in bulk: Validate (n) in the panel of a selection, Validate the selection on right-click, or Validate the N atoms to review on a frame; a confirmation says how many atoms become Validated. A single ⌘Z undoes the batch.
  • Rearrange a frame: after a resize, the map offers Rearrange the cards for a few seconds; the same choice is on the frame's right-click. Cards fit the new width, in reading order, linked cards side by side, and the frame grows if needed.
  • The work behind an atom: an atom's card says “2 open” when epics, stories or changes of its pipe are in progress; the panel lists them under Registry work, each with its title and state, and Open … in the Backlog goes to its page.
  • What is happening: each action says so in a banner at the bottom — while it runs (“Validating 3 atoms…”), then the result (“3 atoms validated: …”) with Undo and Why?, which explains what the action changed; a failure says what to do instead. Log, in the header, keeps every action of the session.
  • Edit: the panel shows only the properties of the element's type. A goal, an actor or a constraint have no pipe, no work nature, no specification; a constraint has its source, a decision its registry decision and date, a milestone its date; solutions (capability, component, module, interface, entity, flow) have everything — specification, documents, work nature (analysis, design, code, test, writing, research, delivery, operations, legal), pipe, horizon (Done · Now · Next · Later), priority. A filled property the type does not show is flagged; Remove them clears it, and ⌘Z brings it back. Nothing is typed when it exists: a document is picked among the project's (or with Browse…, inside the project folder only); work is attached with Attach registry work (epics, stories, tasks, changes, with their state); a decision is picked among the registry's. A cited document that no longer exists is flagged in red. Validate moves an atom to Validated; Back to review returns it to To review.
  • Right click: the menu names the targeted element and sorts its actions by intent. On an atom: View (Open the panel), Status (Validate or Back to review, Horizon ▸), Layout (Back to palette), then Remove from the map, in red. On a link: Nature ▸, Path (Straighten the link, Recentre the label), Remove link. On the background: Add here, a grid of icons, Add ▸ sorted into Intent, Solution and Frame, New group here, Fit all. Submenus open on hover or click; arrows, Enter and Esc work in them.
  • Shortcuts: the ones the menu shows work without it. On the selected atom: V validates (or sends back to review), 1 to 4 set the horizon, Enter opens the panel, ⇧P goes back to the palette, Delete removes. On the map: G creates a group at the centre of the view, ⇧1 fits everything. They stay silent while you type in a field.
  • Style: Header, Fill, Border and Text colours for atoms and frames, and the shape of their border: Border line (Solid, Dashed, Dotted, Double) and Width (Auto, 0.5 to 6); Line colour, Line and Width for links. A click on a swatch opens the palette: suggested swatches, a colour picker, hexadecimal entry, and Half-tone, which softens the chosen colour (for instance a fill in the header's colour, but gentler). Back to palette clears the element's own colour.
  • Options: Display options, next to the zoom, opens the panel that sets up the whole map, for everyone. Reading: size of Titles and Texts (S, M, L), Contrast normal or high. Links: Curvature of the links, and Links under blocks visible (you can make them out through faded cards) or hidden. Colours: Palette picks the look of the whole map — Default, Sober, High contrast, Pastel, or one of My palettes (created in Preferences › Appearance), which is then copied into the project so the team and the agent see it; if you change it later, Update from my palettes brings in the new version. The map follows the app's light or dark theme. A colour set by hand on an element wins over the palette. Each setting is undone by ⌘Z, and SVG, PNG and PDF exports keep it.

What the agent proposes ​

When the agent proposes a change, the N agent proposals pill appears at the top. A click puts the map in preview: a brass outline surrounds it, and a floating bar states the proposal — its summary, then what the batch would do, in plain words (« add 2 layers, 1 atom; change 1 atom »). Nothing is written on the map: what would be added appears dashed green, marked added; what would be changed is ringed, marked changed; what would be removed is struck through. Detail lists each change in one sentence; the arrows move from one proposal to the next. Accept moves the ontology to the next version; Reject… asks for a reason; Leave preview brings back today's map.

A proposal that destroys something — merging two atoms, removing an atom, a link or a layer, taking a card out of one of your groups — says so first: a red What disappears box lists each loss (the atom, its links, its registry citations, the group its card leaves). Such a proposal is never accepted automatically, even in full mode: Accept asks for confirmation, then Accept anyway.

Designing the architecture ​

A component carries a technical nature — user interface (screen, window, popup, form, widget), execution (service, scheduled task, CLI), exchanges (API, external integration) or foundation (library, database). A module stands for a stack of the project: in its panel, Stack picks it from the list, or Declare a stack… creates it; the stack's recent activity shows there, and a module with no work attached follows the progress of the components it contains. Design links: a component realises a capability, a module contains components (a screen can contain forms), a component exposes or calls an interface, persists a data entity, and an entity references another.

To handle ​

To handle says each item in one sentence, with the button that settles it: Set horizon to « Now » when the horizon contradicts the progress, Remove reference when the pipe cites an epic that no longer exists, Attach when an open epic is attached to no atom (choose the atom, the epic enters its pipe), Link to a stack for a module that has none, Create the component for a capability nothing realises, Fill in its pipe or Open for the rest. Locate brings the atom to the centre of the map. Each button is a gesture like any other: ⌘Z undoes it.

Roadmap ​

The Map · Roadmap switch, in the header, sorts capabilities, components, modules, milestones and goals into lanes: one row per layer of the Map, one column per horizon — Now, Next, Later, No horizon; Done (N), collapsed on the left, gathers what is done. Read “when” along the row, and “in which order” down a cell. Each card has the Map's drawing (header, icon, progress, open work); dependencies are drawn between cards, in red when late.

  • Prioritise: drag a card to another column of its lane to change its horizon, or within its cell to change its rank; on the keyboard, ⌥ + arrows. Dragging to another lane is refused: the layer is changed in the panel. Each move is undone by ⌘Z.
  • Ask for arbitration files the request: the agent sees it the next time it reads the Map (and the sentence to say to it is copied). It reads the roadmap, its dependencies and the open work, and proposes moves; it also does so on its own when a lane has nothing left “now”. The proposal opens as a preview on the roadmap — each card at its new place, with “came from …”. In Detail, a move you do not want can be removed (×); then Accept or Reject….
  • Update on closure: when an epic, a story or a task closes (or a change is resolved), what becomes done moves to the Done horizon by itself; the change is recorded in the History (author “registre”) and announced in the banner.
  • Dependencies: a card placed earlier than something it depends on says so (“Depends on …, planned later”); Bring this dependency forward too moves it to the same column, in a single gesture.
  • Create the epic: name it, Create; the epic is created in the registry, already attached to the atom.
  • Apply to the registry gives the open changes of each card the priority of its column (Now 1, Next 2, Later 3), after a confirmation that lists the changes.

History ​

History lists the versions, most recent first, each with its summary, author, date and number of changes. A click on a version shows it on the map: what it changed and still exists is ringed, what has gone since appears dashed. Close preview brings back today's map.

Export writes the map as SVG, PNG, PDF (vector, on one A4 or A3 landscape page, with title, version and date), Mermaid or OWL / RDF (Turtle).

A project without an ontology offers Start empty, or Build from the project: the map is then built by the agent, and the screen gives you the sentence to say.

With an AI agent ​

The agent reads and proposes through the effortless_ontology_* verbs; it never changes the schema directly, it proposes, and it may arrange the map — its last arrangement can be undone here. It is also the agent that attaches registry decisions to step ⑥: it keeps a handful of structuring ones and proposes them to you as one batch, which you accept or reject. See Keep the ontology.
