On this page

Map ​

This screen is the map of your project: its development axes, arranged in layers and linked to each other, with their progress computed from the register. You come here to see at a glance where the project comes from, where it stands and where it is going, to arrange the map your way, and to settle what the agent proposes. It opens from Map, in the « Steering » group of the sidebar.

What you see ​

The screen reads in two zones. At the top, steering: the current version; the N agent proposals pill when the agent awaits an answer; To handle (with the number of items) and History, which open their panel beside the map; Undo the agent's arrangement when the agent has just arranged the map; Export. Left of the map, the element tree: the whole structure of the displayed map, full height — the zones and sub-groups of a layout, or the layers and groups of Custom, each with its number of elements. On the map, the visual: at the top, centred, Search…, the reading (Past, Present, Target) and Filters; the dock at the bottom, centred — atom types as icons (when a panel is open and room runs short, they fold behind Add), then Select, Group (F), Grid, Tour and the layout picker; at the bottom right, the minimap and the zoom — −, the percentage (a click returns to 100 %), +, Fit all and Display options. Each right-hand panel closes with its cross.

On the map:

  • in Custom, a layer is no longer an imposed frame: a layer zone you had placed shows as an ordinary group, which you move, rename, delete or put sub-groups into; a group is a free frame you draw yourself. In a computed layout, frames are rounded zones, one per step of the thread, and the layer is only a discreet sub-group;
  • each atom is an element: its header, marked with its type's icon, is coloured by that type (goal, actor, capability, component, module, interface, data entity, flow, constraint, decision, risk, milestone), with its priority; a component's technical nature (screen, service, API…) shows in a badge, with its icon, next to its type; at the bottom, its review (To review, To frame, Framed, Validated) and its progress, computed from the epics, stories, tasks and changes of its pipe. What is done is shown faded, what is in progress stays solid. A ▲ flags an item to handle: a click on it opens it;
  • each link is a curve from the source atom to the target atom, with its type and nature. Its ends spread along the edges when several links leave the same side.

Filters opens five lists — Layer, Horizon, Progress, Review, Epic — and dims what does not match, hiding nothing: the map keeps its shape. Each active filter becomes a pill next to the button; its cross removes it, and only it. Three readings each answer a question, written next to their buttons: Past — “What have we delivered?” (what is done stays in colour, with the version that delivered it, “delivered in v0.2.47”, or “done, not delivered”); Present — “What is moving forward?” (what is in progress, what carries an alert and what is still To frame); Target — “Where are we going?” (one colour per horizon, and the gaps: aimed at Now but not started, or aimed at without any work). The rest fades to grey. The legend counts each entry; a click on an entry keeps only it, a second click removes it.

What you can do ​

Every gesture is saved at once. ⌘Z undoes it and ⇧⌘Z redoes it, for every gesture of the session.

  • Navigate: the wheel or a pinch zooms around the pointer; hold Space and drag to pan; ⌘0 or ⇧1 fits everything (Fit all), ⌘1 returns to 100 %; the zoom buttons do the same. Zoom and framing glide smoothly; any new gesture interrupts them. The minimap, above the zoom, recentres the view on a click; click and drag to move it live. Seen from afar, each zone shows its name large, at its top left.
  • Select: a click on an element opens its panel; ⇧-click or ⌘-click adds to the selection (or removes from it); a rectangle drawn on the background selects the elements and groups it fully contains, ⇧ or ⌘ adds them to the current selection. Several selected elements, atoms and groups mixed, are styled in one gesture: the style box shows the value they share, or "mixed"; Esc deselects; Delete removes the selection, after a confirmation that names what goes (« Delete 2 atoms and 3 links? »), like every deletion.
  • Arrange: drag an atom or a frame (a frame carries its atoms); a group is grabbed by its header, its background or its border, a layout zone by its border. When selected, a frame shows its size handle in the bottom-right corner. The element stays where you drop it. The map snaps to the grid (Grid, ⌘') and to the edges and centres of other objects; magenta guides show it, and equal gaps between neighbours are measured. Hold ⌥ to suspend snapping. Dropping an atom into a group puts it there; into a layer, changes its layer.
  • Choose a layout: the layout button, at the end of the dock, offers Profiles, Districts, Circles and Nesting, then Custom and your named layouts. Profiles reads the map the way you think about your system, who does what and then what makes it possible: Goals on top, then one column of dedicated features per user type, shared features grouped by who shares them, No profile (capabilities no actor uses yet) and the Common base; each capability forms an island with what delivers it, its lead element marked with a thick border. It is the default layout: the map opens on it while Custom is blank. Districts, Circles and Nesting arrange the map along the design thread — ① Goal → ② Capabilities → ③ Targets → ④ Modules → ⑤ Components → ⑥ Decisions → ⑦ Delivered capabilities, with the Periphery (risks, constraints, resources) and Off the thread (what has no link) apart: Districts as successive districts, Circles as a spiral around the modules, Nesting as nested boxes (Intent, Solution, Decisions). A pale numbered thread links the steps. These layouts are computed on display and write nothing; your choice is kept in your profile and does not change the map for others.
  • Your work first: your groups, their names, their colours and the elements you put in them are shared by every layout. Districts, Circles and Nesting arrange each of your groups as one block, in the step that holds most of its elements; a group without elements goes to Your groups. A computed box you renamed or coloured keeps its name and colour from one layout to the next; when a layout has no matching box, a banner says so, and nothing is lost. Renaming or colouring a group, colouring an element, creating a group apply everywhere, whatever layout is shown.
  • Keep a layout: a layout is created, it never overwrites anything. In a computed layout, Keep this layout, or simply moving an element, creates a layout of your own, “Districts edited”; a banner says so, with Rename and Undo. Custom is the project's layout, shared with the team: it only changes through a gesture made in Custom. Your named layouts are yours alone (they never go into the repository); the selector renames, duplicates or deletes them, and a deletion can be undone from its banner. An element a layout does not place yet arrives next to its neighbours, marked to place, until you set it down.
  • The agent's proposal: when you ask the agent to arrange the map, it creates its own layout, "Proposition de l'agent — <date>", marked "Proposed by the agent" in the picker. It starts from Profiles and takes after your colours and groups but never touches your work or Custom — except on a layout where you ticked Allow the agent to modify it (unticked by default, Custom included): it then arranges it directly, and Undo the agent's arrangement undoes its last one. The agent cannot tick this box itself. A banner says what it proposes (groups, colours, groupings); an element it puts into one of your groups is marked suggestion. Keep all moves the proposal into your work; Keep, in the inspector, keeps one element only; ⌘Z undoes. Delete the proposal removes it.
  • Describe a group: in a group's panel, Metadata lists what the group carries; the + button opens a table where each row picks a metadata Effortless understands, then its value among what the project holds: an epic, a story, a decision, an evolution, a stack, a document, a horizon or a layer. The card on the right says what each metadata is for, what it links to, and what Effortless and the agent do with it. Nothing is invented: an unknown key is refused; an old key that is not understood is kept and flagged. The group shows its metadata at the end of its header (the state of the cited epic, for example), and an element created in the group receives its layer, stack, horizon and the citation of the work in its pipe; the banner says so.
  • Tour: Tour walks the thread step by step (Previous, Next, Quit, or Esc). The step is framed and haloed, the rest fades; a cartouche says what the step brings to the reasoning and, for each element, what it links upstream (a capability, the goal it serves). At step ⑥, each attached decision is read live from the registry: the decision, its motive, the rejected alternatives (struck through, as ghosts) and the periphery forces that weighed in; what is not recorded says so (“no motive recorded”). The tour writes nothing. On an agent proposal whose route it has written, the tour follows that route: one step per moment of the reasoning, in its order, with its title and sentence; each step shows its groups (elements and subgroups included) and the elements it names. The pale thread on the canvas then goes through these steps, numbered like the tour's counter.
  • Add: drag an icon from the dock onto the map, or click it to place the atom at the centre of the view; it is born To frame. The Group (F) tool draws a group: drag on the background to draw a rectangle; the elements it surrounds belong to it, and moving it carries them. Released over a group, the new group becomes its sub-group. While a group is selected, every element added from the dock goes into it, at a free place, and the group stays selected for the next one. A group only touches how the map is presented, never the schema or the versions.
  • Create by linking: pull a handle and release it in empty space; a small window offers the elements compatible with the one you start from (from a component: realises → capability, exposes → interface, persists → entity…). Choose, name it, Create: the element appears there, already linked. ⌘Z removes both.
  • Link: hover an atom, pull one of its four handles to another atom; during the gesture, the atoms that can receive the link light up, the others dim. The New link window offers only the types the rules allow for this pair, minus those already linking the two atoms; choose the nature, a description, then Create link; a click outside closes it.
  • Route a link: select it to see its waypoints; drag them, double-click a segment to add one, a point to remove it; its ends can be dragged to another side. Its label slides along the link, and a little to the side; Recentre the label, on right click, puts it back in its automatic place.
  • Browse the tree: the tree scrolls on its own; its chevrons expand a frame. Choosing an element selects it, centres it and moves a little closer; choosing a frame frames it. The element selected on the canvas is highlighted in the tree. After a click in the tree, with the keyboard, ↑ and ↓ move from one row to the next and show the element reached on the canvas (a group reached unfolds); → expands a frame or enters it, ← folds it or goes up to the parent frame; Home and End go to the first and last row. Drag an element or a group from the tree onto a group to attach it there: it takes a free place, and the group grows if needed. The button in its header folds it into a thin strip, and its right edge drags to widen it (200 to 480 px); width and folding come back when you reopen.
  • Search: ⌘F puts the cursor in Search…; what you type also filters the tree, which keeps only the elements found and their frames, expanded; type a name (accents don't matter), an identifier or a registry reference. Choosing a result selects the atom, centres it and zooms in if it was too small to read.
  • Sort: Filters keeps one layer, horizon, progress, review or epic; Alerts › With an alert keeps only the atoms with something to handle. Each frame shows its number of alerts (▲).
  • Focus, collapse: right-click a frame, Focus on this frame dims the rest (the Focus chip or Esc leave it); the chevron at the start of the title, or Collapse, shrinks the frame to a bar (“4 atoms collapsed”) that its links reach; Expand opens it again. A folded group becomes a block with counted ports, opened by double-click (see "Groups and sub-graphs").
  • Mark reviewed: an element proposed by the agent arrives To review; an element you place yourself is born To frame. Mark reviewed (panel button, V key, right-click) moves it from To review to To frame. In bulk: Mark reviewed (n) in the panel of a selection, Mark selection reviewed on right-click, or Mark N items reviewed on a frame; a confirmation says how many elements move to To frame. A single ⌘Z undoes the batch. An element becomes Framed once work (epic, story, task) is linked to it, and Validated on its own once that work is done. Only a decision or an actor, which never carry work, are validated by hand: Validate moves them from To frame to Validated, Back to framing returns them, and it is also offered for any element validated by hand earlier.
  • Rearrange a frame: after a resize, the map offers Rearrange the elements for a few seconds; the same choice is on the frame's right-click. Elements fit the new width, in reading order, linked elements side by side, and the frame grows if needed.
  • The work behind an atom: an atom says “2 open” when epics, stories or changes of its pipe are in progress; the panel lists them under Registry work, each with its title and state, and Open … in the Backlog goes to its page.
  • What is happening: each action says so in a banner at the bottom — while it runs (“Reviewing 3 items…”), then the result (“3 items reviewed: …”) with Undo and Why?, which explains what the action changed; a failure says what to do instead. Log, in the header, keeps every action of the session.
  • Edit: the panel shows only the properties of the element's type. A goal, an actor or a constraint have no pipe, no work nature, no specification; a constraint has its source, a decision its registry decision and date, a milestone its date, a solution, component or module its Register deliverable (offered only when the project has steering; the element then shows a coloured dot with the rate, see Objectives and milestones); solutions (capability, component, module, interface, entity, flow) have everything — specification, documents, work nature (analysis, design, code, test, writing, research, delivery, operations, legal), pipe, horizon (Done · Now · Next · Later), priority. A filled property the type does not show is flagged; Remove them clears it, and ⌘Z brings it back. Nothing is typed when it exists: a document is picked among the project's (or with Browse…, inside the project folder only); work is attached with Attach registry work (epics, stories, tasks, changes, with their state); a decision is picked among the registry's. A cited document that no longer exists is flagged in red. Mark reviewed, Validate or Back to framing appears depending on the element's state (see Mark reviewed); an element to frame that carries work has no button: its Validated will come from its finished work.
  • Right click: the menu names the targeted element and sorts its actions by intent. On an atom: View (Open the panel), Status (Mark reviewed, Validate or Back to framing depending on the state, Horizon ▸), Layout (Back to palette), Edit (Copy, Duplicate), Maps (Detail in a sub-map, Attach a sub-map, Remove from this map), then Delete the element, in red. On a link: Nature ▸, Path (Straighten the link, Recentre the label), Remove link. On the background: Add here, a grid of icons, Add ▸ sorted into Intent, Solution and Frame, New group here, Paste here (when elements are copied), New map here, Place an existing element here, Fit all. On a group: Copy, Duplicate and Paste here in its Edit section. Submenus open on hover or click; arrows, Enter and Esc work in them.
  • Shortcuts: the ones the menu shows work without it. On the selected atom: V takes the review step shown (mark reviewed, validate a decision or an actor, back to framing), 1 to 4 set the horizon, Enter opens the panel, ⇧P goes back to the palette, Delete removes. On the map: G creates a group at the centre of the view, ⇧1 fits everything. They stay silent while you type in a field.
  • Style: Header, Fill, Border and Text colours for atoms and frames, and the shape of their border: Border line (Solid, Dashed, Dotted, Double) and Width (Auto, 0.5 to 6); Line colour, Line and Width for links. A click on a swatch opens the palette: suggested swatches, a colour picker, hexadecimal entry, and Half-tone, which softens the chosen colour (for instance a fill in the header's colour, but gentler). Back to palette clears the element's own colour.
  • Copy, paste, duplicate: ⌘C copies the selected elements (Ctrl+C on Windows), ⌘V pastes them a little further on, ⌘D duplicates them; from the right-click menu, Copy, Duplicate, and Paste here drops the copies at the point you clicked, inside the group found there. A group is copied with its subgroups and elements; duplicated, it lands just to the right of the original. A copy keeps the element's type, description and fields, but neither its backlog references nor its review state: it starts over To frame. Links between elements copied together are recreated; a link to an element that is not copied is not. A single ⌘Z removes the paste. In a text field, these keys keep their usual meaning.
  • Spell checking: in a text field (title, description…), a misspelled word is underlined according to your system settings; right-click opens the system menu, with its suggestions, Cut, Copy and Paste.
  • Options: Display options, next to the zoom, opens the panel that sets up the whole map, for everyone. Reading: size of Titles and Texts (S, M, L), Contrast normal or high. Links: Curvature of the links, and Links under blocks visible (you can make them out through faded elements) or hidden. Colours: Palette picks the look of the whole map — Default, Sober, High contrast, Pastel, or one of My palettes (created in Preferences › Appearance), which is then copied into the project so the team and the agent see it; if you change it later, Update from my palettes brings in the new version. The map follows the app's light or dark theme. A colour set by hand on an element wins over the palette. Each setting is undone by ⌘Z, and SVG, PNG and PDF exports keep it.

What the agent proposes ​

When the agent proposes a change, the N agent proposals pill appears at the top. A click puts the map in preview: a brass outline surrounds it, and a floating bar states the proposal — its summary, then what the batch would do, in plain words (« add 2 layers, 1 atom; change 1 atom »). Nothing is written on the map: what would be added appears dashed green, marked added; what would be changed is ringed, marked changed; what would be removed is struck through. Detail lists each change in one sentence; the arrows move from one proposal to the next. Accept moves the ontology to the next version; Reject… asks for a reason; Leave preview brings back today's map.

A proposal that destroys something — merging two atoms, removing an atom, a link or a layer, taking an element out of one of your groups — says so first: a red What disappears box lists each loss (the atom, its links, its registry citations, the group its element leaves). Such a proposal is never accepted automatically, even in full mode: Accept asks for confirmation, then Accept anyway.

Designing the architecture ​

A component carries a technical nature — user interface (screen, window, popup, form, widget), execution (service, scheduled task, CLI), exchanges (API, external integration) or foundation (library, database). A module stands for a stack of the project: in its panel, Stack picks it from the list, or Declare a stack… creates it; the stack's recent activity shows there, and a module with no work attached follows the progress of the components it contains. Design links: a component realises a capability, a module contains components (a screen can contain forms), a component exposes or calls an interface, persists a data entity, and an entity references another.

To handle ​

To handle says each item in one sentence, with the button that settles it: Set horizon to « Now » when the horizon contradicts the progress, Remove reference when the pipe cites an epic that no longer exists, Attach when an open epic is attached to no atom (choose the atom, the epic enters its pipe), Link to a stack for a module that has none, Create the component for a capability nothing realises, Fill in its pipe or Open for the rest. Locate brings the atom to the centre of the map. Each button is a gesture like any other: ⌘Z undoes it.

On a sub-map, the list keeps only the items of elements placed on that map — a neighbour shown in Context is not one of them. The whole design shows every item; opening another map restores the filter. The alerts themselves are always computed on the whole design.

Roadmap ​

The Map · Roadmap switch, in the header, sorts capabilities, components, modules, milestones and goals into lanes: one row per layer of the Map, one column per horizon — Now, Next, Later, No horizon; Done (N), collapsed on the left, gathers what is done. Read “when” along the row, and “in which order” down a cell. On a sub-map, the roadmap shows only the elements placed on that map; The whole design shows them all. An element prioritised here takes its rank among the whole column, hidden elements included. Each element has the Map's drawing (header, icon, progress, open work); dependencies are drawn between elements, in red when late.

  • Prioritise: drag an element to another column of its lane to change its horizon, or within its cell to change its rank; on the keyboard, ⌥ + arrows. Dragging to another lane is refused: the layer is changed in the panel. Each move is undone by ⌘Z.
  • Ask for arbitration files the request: the agent sees it the next time it reads the Map (and the sentence to say to it is copied). It reads the roadmap, its dependencies and the open work, and proposes moves; it also does so on its own when a lane has nothing left “now”. The proposal opens as a preview on the roadmap — each element at its new place, with “came from …”. In Detail, a move you do not want can be removed (×); then Accept or Reject….
  • Update on closure: when an epic, a story or a task closes (or a change is resolved), what becomes done moves to the Done horizon by itself; the change is recorded in the History (author “registre”) and announced in the banner.
  • Dependencies: an element placed earlier than something it depends on says so (“Depends on …, planned later”); Bring this dependency forward too moves it to the same column, in a single gesture.
  • Create the epic: name it, Create; the epic is created in the registry, already attached to the atom.
  • Apply to the registry gives the open changes of each element the priority of its column (Now 1, Next 2, Later 3), after a confirmation that lists the changes.

Groups and sub-graphs ​

A group folds into a block, and opens like a sub-graph: on a large map you see the project at one level, then enter a group to see only its contents. A group you create (New group here, the G key, or drawn with the frame tool) opens its panel with the cursor in its Title: type its name.

  • Folded group: the group becomes a block with its title, its number of elements and a chevron. On its edges, one port per neighbour and per direction, with the number of links ("7 → Loop"); hovering lists the link types ("uses ×3, realises ×4"). Links between two folded blocks merge into a single counted line.
  • Fold and unfold: the group's chevron, or Collapse and Expand on right-click. On the Dock, Fold all and Unfold all act on every group at once; each is undone with ⌘Z. On the whole Map, Fold all gives the overview: one block per top-level group. A group that only wraps a single other group is passed through: the first level with several groups is folded.
  • Enter a group: double-click its header, or its folded block. The Map then shows only its contents, sub-groups included, in place and with their current positions. Links to the outside become doors on the edge: a click on a door enters the neighbouring group, or returns to the whole Map if that neighbour is outside any group ("Ungrouped").
  • Going back: the breadcrumb, top left, shows where you are (Maps › Main map › Group › Sub-group); a click on a level returns there, and Escape goes up one level.
  • Search: choosing an element inside a folded group opens that group and selects the element.

Entering a group changes nothing: it is a screen, not a write. Gestures made inside (moving, styling) are written to the layout, as everywhere. Each layout remembers the level where you left it. With no group at all, the Dock does not show these commands, and the Map stays the one you know.

Automatic arrangement ​

The Map can arrange the elements of a group for you: you keep control of the order and the content, it takes care of the positions. The examples follow the fictitious workshop project (Quote, Issued invoice, Reminder).

  • Three levels, in the Options panel: Manual (nothing is arranged), Semi-auto (default: existing elements stay put, elements you create afterwards are arranged) and Auto (every element is arranged, including those on the map background: no resize handles, automatic link routes, height following the title). The level, the spacing and the grid step apply to the whole Map: set on the main map they apply to the sub-maps, and you can set them from any map.
  • Per element: the Automatic arrangement option in the style panel decides whether a block is arranged. In Auto it is greyed out (“imposed by the global setting”). Place an existing element here does not arrange the placed element.
  • Rows or grid: an arranged group is shown as a Grid (default) or as Rows. In a grid, the number of columns is Auto (from the group width) or fixed to N; in rows, the horizontal alignment positions each row. The vertical alignment applies in both modes. In a grid, elements fill their column, so horizontal alignment is greyed out.
  • Spacing: group padding (left, top, right, bottom), margin around each element, and gap between elements (horizontal, vertical). A group can override these values for itself.
  • Sizes: the height of an arranged element follows its title (never below the standard height); the height you had chosen for it is kept and comes back once it is no longer arranged (in Semi-auto, for instance); an arranged group hugs its content in height; its width handle changes the number of columns. Arranged elements have no resize handles.
  • Dragging: dragging “Reminder” into an arranged group makes its neighbours move aside one place to show where it will land; on release it takes that place in a single gesture, undoable with ⌘Z. ⌥ + arrows moves the selected element by one place. An element that leaves the group for the map background snaps to the grid.
  • Links: the links of an arranged element use the automatic route; their manual waypoints are kept and come back if arrangement is turned off.
  • Turning a group's arrangement off freezes its elements where they are (undoable). Computed layouts (Profiles, Districts, Circles, Nesting) are not arranged by this setting.
  • Animations: moves last about 0.2 s; they are off when Reduce motion is on in macOS.

Several maps ​

A single map ends up too big to read. You can split the design into batches: the main map gives the broad lines, and sub-maps each detail one scope. There is still only one design: an element can appear on several maps without being duplicated. You see one map at a time, and move from one to the next by navigating. The examples below follow a fictitious workshop-management project, with a “Billing” sub-map.

  • Create a map: on right-click, New map here on the background, New sub-map in this group on a group, Attach a sub-map on an element; or, in the maps tree, New sub-map… in a map's menu. A name and an objective are asked for (“Billing” — “Issue invoices and follow up reminders”). What you clicked becomes the sub-map's anchor: an element, a group, or the map itself.
  • Detail in a sub-map: select elements, or a group, then Detail in a sub-map (or Detail this group in a sub-map) on right-click. The sub-map is born with those elements, at their position — a group keeps its layout there. On the parent map, they are removed and a map element takes their place. A single ⌘Z undoes it all.
  • The map element tells at a glance the sub-map's name and objective, and what it brings: the number of elements per type, sub-maps included, and the share already done. Its links to the rest are grouped and counted, like those of a folded group (“3 → Customer”). A double-click opens the sub-map. A sub-map attached to an element leaves a ⤵ mark on it: hovering names the sub-map, a double-click opens it.
  • Navigate: the breadcrumb starts with Maps, then chains the maps and the groups (Maps › Main map › Billing › Reminders). A click on Maps shows the maps tree in the left panel: the main map, and each sub-map under its map, its group or its anchor element. A click on a map opens it, and the panel returns to the map's elements. A row's menu renames the map, changes its objective, moves it (a new anchor), deletes it, or makes it the agent map.
  • Up, Siblings: inside a sub-map, a strip under the breadcrumb offers Up to the parent map and, when the sub-map shares a group with others, its Siblings, which open with a click.
  • Context: the elements of other maps linked to the sub-map are gathered in a single Context element, which names them with their map. A click on a name opens its map, with the element selected. Drag the handle at its bottom-right corner to set its width: a long name wraps and the height follows. Map element, Up, Siblings and Context are for navigating: they count neither in the alerts nor in the roadmap.
  • Place, remove: Place an existing element here, on the background's right-click, searches the whole design and adds the element to the displayed map. Remove from this map takes it off without deleting it. An element that is on no map any more shows in the Off map branch, at the bottom of the tree: Place on the map… puts it back on the displayed map. Nothing is lost.
  • Delete a map: the confirmation says what comes back to the parent map — its elements that were on no other map — and where its sub-maps are re-attached. The elements are not deleted. If a sub-map's anchor element or group disappears, the sub-map sits under its parent map, marked reattached.
  • Groups, layouts: each map has its own groups, positions and layouts; an element on two maps has a place on each. A sub-map's palette and display options follow the main map until you set them on the sub-map.
  • The agent map: the agent knows the whole design, but works first on the map you point it to, otherwise on the agent map, marked agent in the tree (the main map until you pick another). What it adds or proposes lands on that map, and it says so. It can also propose a new map, to isolate a concept; you accept or reject it like any proposal.
  • Export several maps: Export › Several maps… opens the maps tree with checkboxes (Check all, Uncheck all). In SVG and PNG, one file per checked map, in the folder you choose; in PDF, a single file, one map per page; in Mermaid and OWL, the elements of each map, with their links to other maps noted as references. Each file name carries its map's name. Current map exports the displayed map, as before.

A project without sub-maps does not change: its main map holds everything, and nothing is rewritten until you create a sub-map.

History ​

History lists the versions, most recent first, each with its summary, author, date and number of changes. A click on a version shows it on the map: what it changed and still exists is ringed, what has gone since appears dashed. Close preview brings back today's map.

Export writes the current map as SVG, PNG, PDF (vector, on one A4 or A3 landscape page, with title, version and date), Mermaid or OWL / RDF (Turtle); for several maps, see “Several maps”.

A project without an ontology offers Start empty, or Build from the project: the map is then built by the agent, and the screen gives you the sentence to say.

With an AI agent ​

The agent reads and proposes through the effortless_ontology_* verbs; it never changes the schema directly, it proposes, and it may arrange the map — its last arrangement can be undone here. It is also the agent that attaches registry decisions to step ⑥: it keeps a handful of structuring ones and proposes them to you as one batch, which you accept or reject. See Keep the ontology.
